## Answer in one paragraph

The six-target ceiling is a **spatial confinement**, not a target-identity or memory problem. The three targets the
policies never break are exactly the three targets left of the stage's tall wall (native IDs 1, 6, 8): **0 of 3,074
replayed action sequences (5,797 historical rows) broke any of them, and in not one of them did Mario ever go further
left than x = -1650**, which is Mario standing against the wall's right face (x = -1800). That holds for every seed,
every checkpoint including the untrained initial policies, M7d and M7e, v1 and v2, stochastic and deterministic
policies, random play and the selection-bias sample. Right of the wall there are seven targets; the policies take the
five central ones reliably, the right target (ID 3) sometimes, and the moving upper-right target (ID 2) almost never
(1.1 % of sequences). A six-target episode is therefore "all seven right-of-wall targets but one" — in 65 of 72
six-target rows the missing one is the moving target — and the seven-target set that needs no wall crossing was never
achieved. No evidence points to route ambiguity: the remaining set is nearly always the same.

## 1. Direct native facts

* The three left-of-wall targets (IDs 1, 6, 8) were broken in **0 of 3,074** replayed sequences (0 of 5,797 rows);
  every other ID was broken at least 33 times. IDs ever broken: 0, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9.
* **No replayed episode ever had Mario at x < -1650.** 69.4 % of sequences pressed against the wall face (x = -1650);
  none entered the wall column or the region beyond it.
* Only 14 sequences (0.46 %) ever reached wall-top height (y >= 2990, up to y 4645). Stepwise re-replay: none was ever
  grounded on the wall-top ledge (x -2100..-1200 at y 3000), and while at that height none came closer to the wall
  than x = -357. The highest episodes were untrained initial-policy episodes (max y 4645).
* Six-target rows (72 rows, 66 sequences): `{0,3,4,5,7,9}` x 65, `{0,2,3,4,5,9}` x 4, `{0,2,4,5,7,9}` x 3. The
  remaining four: `{1,2,6,8}` in 65 rows (90.3 %), `{1,6,7,8}` in 4, `{1,3,6,8}` in 3.
* The seven-target set reachable without crossing the wall, `{0,2,3,4,5,7,9}`, was never broken in one episode.
* The moving target (ID 2) broke in 33 sequences (1.1 %); the right target (ID 3) in 490 (15.9 %); ID 7 in 627
  (20.4 %); IDs 0, 4, 9 in 94-97 %; ID 5 in 69 %.
* Same-tick double breaks resolve in ascending ID order (e.g. IDs 0 and 4 at consumed tick 483), as the item process
  queue order predicts; every break's native `break_input_tick` equals `consumed_tick + 1`.
* The 7.43 TAS, for reference, breaks `9, 4, 5, 2, 7, 3, 0, 6, 8, 1`: it climbs onto the wall-top ledge (y 3000 at
  tick 316), passes over the wall at y ~3400-3500 (ticks 340-358), descends on the left (min x -4071) and uses 9 B
  presses; several targets break while Mario is far away from them (projectiles).

## 2. Statistically supported patterns

* **Left targets are the three most-missed targets in every population** (M7e final stochastic miss order
  `1, 6, 8, 2, 3, 7, 9, 5, 0, 4`). With 3,074 independent sequences and zero breaks, the per-sequence rate of breaking
  any left target is below 0.1 % (rule of three) in the evidence available.
* **The fourth missed target is the moving target (ID 2).** M7e final stochastic: ID 2 broken in 1.0 % (95 % CI
  0.3-2.9 %), ID 3 in 16.3 % (12.6-20.9 %), ID 7 in 49.3 % (43.7-55.0 %). A seventh target would require ID 2 and ID 3
  and all five central targets in one episode; ID 2 and ID 3 co-occur in 18 of 5,797 rows, never together with ID 7.
* **One confinement, several incomplete route families inside it.** 13 distinct broken subsets reach >= 5 targets
  across the replayed corpus, all within the seven right-of-wall targets. The M7e seeds differ in *which* of the
  optional right-side targets they take: seed 1 almost always takes central ID 7 (94 %) and almost never the right
  target (1 %); seeds 0 and 2 take the right target more often (27 %, 21 %) and ID 7 less (46 %, 8 %).
* **Ordering is stable**: the first break is ID 0 (50.7 % of M7e final rows, median tick 34 when first), ID 9
  (25.7 %) or ID 4 (20.7 %); the central floor/column targets follow (`0->9`, `9->4`, `4->5` are the common
  transitions); ID 7 (median tick 1,559), ID 3 (2,240) and ID 2 (2,033) come late; the median gap between breaks is
  246 ticks.
* **Long no-progress tails end after a central target**: 61.7 % of M7e final stochastic episodes break their first
  target by tick 600 and then make no progress for >= 1,800 ticks; their last target is ID 5 (73), 7 (39), 0 (27),
  4 (18), 9 (17), 3 (10), 2 (1) — i.e. the policy runs out of reachable targets and keeps exploring the same region
  (98 % of all M7e final steps are in the central region, 2 % right of x 2100, 0 % left of the wall).
* **Training shifted behaviour toward the central targets, not across the wall.** Untrained initial policies:
  mean 3.24 targets, ID 2 in 2.3 %, ID 3 in 8.3 %, max 5; final: mean 4.58, ID 5 97 %, ID 7 49 %, ID 3 16 %, ID 2
  1 %. No checkpoint of any seed ever broke a left target (trajectory table). Later checkpoints did not acquire any
  previously unseen target: the seven right-of-wall IDs the final policies break (0, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9) were all
  already broken by the untrained policies, and ID 2 is broken *less* often after training than before.
* **Stochastic vs deterministic**: the argmax policies are far weaker and collapsed: M7e final deterministic breaks
  `[0, 9]`, `[0, 9]`, `[0, 4, 9]` (seeds 0, 1, 2); M7d final deterministic 0-3 targets. Across all 1,961
  deterministic rows no policy broke a left target, the moving target or ID 7, or more than 4 targets (IDs ever
  broken: 0, 3, 4, 5, 9).
* **M7d vs M7e / v1 vs v2**: all confined identically (min x -1650 everywhere). Pooled v1 policies go right far more
  often (enter x > 2100 in 93 % of M7d v1 final rows; ID 3 in 53.7 %) but miss the central ID 5 (35 %) and ID 7
  (6 %). At seed 0, where v1 beat v2 in M7d (4.31 vs 3.95 targets), the difference is spread over right-of-wall
  targets — ID 3 +0.22, ID 5 +0.16, ID 7 +0.10, ID 2 +0.05 per episode — not any new region.
* **Selection bias check**: the hash sample of otherwise unselected replayable sequences (200 sequences) shows the same
  structure (0 left breaks, min x -1650, ID 2 in 0 %, max 5 targets), so the targeted strata are not unrepresentative
  of the replayable evidence on these questions.

## 3. Plausible interpretations (not established causally)

* The tall wall is a navigation barrier the learned and random policies never pass. Crossing it requires getting onto
  the wall-top ledge (y 3000, x -2100..-1200) or over the wall at y > 3000, a multi-step climbing manoeuvre whose
  intermediate states were never visited (closest approach at that height: x = -357). Because no left target was
  ever broken in training, PPO never received any reward from that region and has no gradient pointing to it — an
  exploration / credit-assignment problem.
* Identity information would not change this: the remaining set is almost fully determined by `targets_remaining`
  and position on this route family (the remaining four are the same set in 90 % of six-target rows), and no
  observation or memory can produce reward from a region that is never reached.
* Entropy alone is unlikely to help: the near-maximum-entropy untrained policies (300 episodes) and random play were
  exactly as confined, and they reached the greatest heights without ever approaching the wall top at height.
* The moving target is a second, smaller barrier: it rides 600 units above the moving platform at x = 2700 and is
  broken mostly late in an episode (median tick ~2,000), consistent with a rare coincidence of height and phase.

## 4. Unknowns

* Whether the left region is reachable with Track 1 actions (MultiDiscrete([9, 8]); the TAS uses 8 intermediate
  analog rows and fireballs). Heights above the wall top (y 4645) were reached with Track 1 actions, and the ledge is
  an ordinary standing surface, so reachability is likely but not demonstrated here — and M7f must not approximate
  the TAS through Track 1 to test it.
* Whether left targets can be hit from the right side (projectiles over the wall): no replayed episode did it.
* The 4,685 non-replayable rows (actions never stored: 4,491 training, 194 random baseline) cannot be
  position-checked; their summaries never exceed 6 targets, which is consistent with, but does not prove, the same
  confinement.
* Nothing here says which intervention will cross the wall; see the proposal for the controlled test.

## Limitations

* Diagnosis of recorded behaviour only; no training was run and nothing here is causal.
* The replayed rows are 5,797 of 11,767 (49 %); the replayable population is 7,082 rows (60 %: every evaluation row
  except 194 random-baseline rows, plus 533 preserved training artifacts); the other 4,685 rows are reported, not
  replayed.
* Regions are labels from the stage collision data applied to native positions; the ID rule does not depend on them.
* Seeds and budgets are those of M7d / M7e only (one machine, one task, Mario US).

## Post-analysis project decision (addendum, recorded after the analysis)

This addendum records a project decision made after the analysis above. It changes no diagnostic result, table or
count in this report; nothing here was re-measured and the regression chain was not rerun.

* **Reported by the user (not yet repository-validated):** both wall crossings are achievable using only the existing
  `btt_s9_b8_v1` Track 1 actions — (1) the precise lower-platform jump, and (2) the moving-platform-assisted upper
  crossing. This rules out a known action-space impossibility and bears directly on the unknown in section 4
  ("whether the left region is reachable with Track 1 actions"), which stays open in the repository until the
  fixtures below validate. It is **not** a repository-validated fact: no canonical Track 1 action sequence for either
  crossing has been captured or replayed.
* **Next milestone, first step:** capture and validate one Track 1-only fixture per crossing, each starting at tick 0
  and replayed natively under the usual rules (fresh process, canonical native actions, exact consumed-tick
  reproduction, diagnostic evidence that Mario reaches x < -2100 through the named crossing). Until both fixtures
  validate, the crossings remain a reported claim.
* **Use restriction:** the crossing fixtures are validation evidence only. They must not be used as PPO
  demonstrations, curriculum start states, archive seeds, reward shaping or any other training signal.
* **Order of work:** because the project decided that the agent should perceive visible stage information, a
  structured observation v2 will be designed and compared (a new, separately versioned observation contract; the
  15-value `btt_policy_obs_v1` stays frozen) before the proposed frontier curriculum is launched.
* **The M7f recommendation stands in the record.** The evidence-based category-D recommendation
  (`docs/rl_next_experiment_m7f_proposal.md`) is unchanged; the curriculum is **deferred, not erased**, and remains a
  candidate after the observation-v2 comparison.
