# M7t synthetic readout test (2026-09-29)

**Pinned outcome: CONFIRM.** On the saved synthetic T policies, replacing the goal-node readout with a set readout
over training-archive states raised returns to the registered rare goals from **28 / 56 to 46 / 56**. That is 18 goals
gained and none lost, and no seed got worse.

**The narrow claim this supports:** a different goal readout improves these saved synthetic policies. It does **not**
show that training without goal nodes works, that the optimizer converged, or anything about Mario. It authorises no
gate.

**Unchanged:**
- the registered synthetic prerequisite FAIL (S1-S4, `docs/rl_m7t_synthetic_prerequisite_2026-09-29.md`) and its
  review;
- the M7o, M7r and M7s outcomes;
- v3 + reward v2 as the selected baseline;
- every observation, reward and action contract.

**Not done:** no BattleShip launch, no native tick, no optimizer step, no new network, no commit or push.

- **Pin.** `pin` wrote `logs/m7t_synth_readout/protocol.json` (write-once, Git-ignored) at **18:41:27Z**:
  - protocol digest **`800a2544b10462a3…`**;
  - tool sha256 `1001f14e…`, identical at pin and at run;
  - the run started at 18:41:37Z.
- **Pinned inputs by sha256:** the saved networks `s{0,1,2}_T.pt`, the registered `results.json` and episode files, the
  harness and `m7s_goal.py`.
- **Before the pin**, only three things happened:
  - a count of training-archive members per goal (every goal had ≥ 2);
  - a change that keeps only evaluation-relevant data per seed, to respect the 2 GB cap;
  - a check of the pair-indexing arithmetic on plain random tensors (no network, no outcome).

| item | pinned value |
| --- | --- |
| readout O (original) | d(T(z_t, a), w(G)) with the saved goal encoder |
| readout R (the one change) | min over i of d(T(z_t, a), z(g_i)): g_i are at most 32 full states of cell G, z the saved state encoder |
| representatives | training-split rows of the registered data (regenerated by seed) whose valid cell equals G, in ascending sha256("m7t_readout\|rep\|seed\|episode\|k") order, first 32. Held-out episodes, evaluation trajectories and their states are never in the pool. Selection finished for all seeds before any evaluation |
| fixed | the 72 words; torch.argmin (lowest index on ties); the same predicted next latents T(z, a) for both readouts; start; 3,600-tick budget; batch = all goals of a seed in registered order; 6 threads; no_grad; no optimizer object |
| goals | every registered rare goal E: 16 + 20 + 20 = **56**, one greedy episode per goal and readout |
| baseline gate | readout O must reproduce every saved registered episode (words, end reason, reach tick) before R runs; otherwise stop |
| caps | 7,200 s wall; 2,048 MB peak private memory; 0 optimizer steps; a breach stops the run with no interpretation |

**Decision rule (pinned):**

| outcome | condition |
| --- | --- |
| **confirm** | n_R ≥ 42, losses g− ≤ 3, and n_R ≥ n_O in every seed |
| **reject** | n_R ≤ n_O + 4 |
| inconclusive | anything else |

**Justification (pinned):**
- 42 is 75 % of 56. It means recovering half of the 28 control failures, the review's 36 / 48 scaled to all goals.
- g− ≤ 3 prevents a gain that trades away previously reached goals.
- "No seed worse" prevents one seed carrying the result.
- The reject bound of control + 4 reflects dependent goal outcomes (one route reaches several clustered goals) and
  reuses the gate rule's twin margin.
- The sign test is descriptive only.

Reading the diagnostics:
- **Every remaining failure is still a false arrival.** R reduces the collapse (28 failures → 10) but does not remove
  it. Its 10 failures stall at states 13-30 true ticks away, with the readout at or below 1.
- **R reaches more goals, not faster.** On goals both readouts reached, R was slower in 20 of 28. More of its successful
  episodes pass through a premature near-zero plateau before arriving (15 against 11).
- **No failure under either readout is simple horizon exhaustion.**

## 4. What this supports, and its limits

**Supported.** For these three saved T networks, measuring goal distance as the minimum over representative
training-archive states of the goal cell, instead of the learned goal node, recovers most goal-node stalls on airborne
rare goals. This is consistent with the review's finding that the goal-node readout collapses at short range while
state-to-state distances do not.

**Not supported, and not claimed:**
- **That training without goal nodes works.** These networks were trained *with* goal nodes and cost-0 membership,
  which shaped the state geometry R reads.
- **Optimizer convergence.** Nothing was trained, and the registered constraint was unconverged.
- **A repair of the collapse.** R still collapses: all 10 of its failures are false arrivals.
- **Speed.** R is slower on commonly reached goals, so it is no better for time-to-goal or stitching under a budget.
  S2 was not the evaluation set.
- **The gate-design problems.** The execution diagnostics, the K reference and F3 remain unrepaired.
- **Anything about Mario or any gate.** The world is a small discrete synthetic platformer, and R needs an archive of
  real member states per goal.

**Sample.** 56 dependent goals from three networks. A confirm is a large paired effect on these saved policies, not a
rate estimate.

**Archive dependence.** R uses states from the frozen training split. Goals with few members (2-9) still had
representatives; a goal with none could not use R at all.
